# Coverage Reconciliation Method

E-tally records are at `campaign_date + team_id + settlement_id` grain. A linked row is reported service evidence; it is not proof of physical arrival. The baseline GPS visit classification is physical-presence evidence; it is not proof of vaccination.

Settlement coverage uses planned settlements as the denominator. GPS coverage is confirmed GPS visits divided by planned settlements; e-tally coverage is linked reported settlements divided by planned settlements. Reconciliation classes retain visited, unvisited, and ambiguous GPS evidence separately.

Unmatched IDs are retained outside the linked raw table. Duplicate reporting keys are reported explicitly. Raw doses are preserved; totals report all linked doses and plausible-only doses excluding rows above their reported target population.

Ward severity as implemented (`src/reconciliation/ward_coverage.py`) is a binary operational flag, not the graded percentage-point rule this section previously described: a ward is `requires review` if any settlement inside it carries a definitive GPS/e-tally disagreement, otherwise `aligned`. **Correction, 2026-07-31:** this section previously described a three-tier low/moderate/high scheme keyed to `absolute_percentage_point_difference` that was never actually implemented in code -- a documentation/implementation mismatch found and fixed during the same audit that found the GPS date-mismatch defect, unrelated to it. All 40 wards currently carry `requires review` (every ward has at least one disagreement at the corrected coverage level), so this binary flag no longer differentiates for triage purposes; the Incident Manager brief instead ranks wards by `absolute_percentage_point_difference` for a phased verification order. Present the reconciled product to the Incident Manager. GPS is stronger evidence of presence; e-tally is stronger evidence of reported delivery and doses. Neither alone proves both.

Strict agreement is GPS visited/e-tally reported plus GPS unvisited/no e-tally report. Definitive agreement rate is agreement divided by agreement plus disagreement, excluding ambiguous GPS cases. Execution found 661 agreements, 1,657 disagreements, and 244 ambiguous cases (28.52% definitive agreement; fully revised 2026-07-31 after four compounding GPS-pipeline defects were found and fixed, documented in `technical_decisions.md` — originally 742/1,640/180 at 31.15%). The 73.26 percentage-point GPS/e-tally coverage gap is operationally substantial (originally 70.34). Of the disagreements, 1,654 settlements were e-tally reported but GPS unvisited and 3 were GPS visited without e-tally reporting. These findings do not alone prove false reporting: logger non-use or failure, poor capture, identifier or timing mismatch, and genuine reporting without corroborating GPS evidence remain plausible.

A gap this wide is itself informative. e-tally reporting stayed at 78.69% (untouched by the GPS-side fixes), which is not plausibly consistent with a genuine 5.43% physical-presence rate -- if only around one in twenty settlements had actually been visited, dose administration on the scale e-tally reports would not have been possible. The far more defensible reading is that GPS-confirmed visit evidence is data-limited: even after all four defects were corrected, only a fraction of the raw 956,702 points are trustworthy within the true campaign window, and a confirmed visit still requires a sustained 3-point/15-minute episode from that reduced pool. Treat the 5.43% figure as a lower bound on confirmed GPS evidence, not as an estimate of true coverage, and verify disagreements before mop-up rather than acting on either raw figure.
