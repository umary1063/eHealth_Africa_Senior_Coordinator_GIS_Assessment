# Question 6: Building Capability in the Counterpart Agency

Compulsory, Part 3, maximum 6 pages excluding annexes.

**[Q6_Response.docx](Q6_Response.docx)** is the full response: an evidence-to-design-implication
table reading the capability assessment (composite 36/100, objective mean 57%, a 0.11 self-
rating/tested-knowledge correlation, and zero of 21 staff with QGIS/ArcGIS access) directly into
course design rather than into the cohort's own stated demand; a four-level, six-domain
competency framework (Annex A) built on observable behaviours, not a generic beginner/
intermediate/advanced scale; a seven-column, day-by-day course table (learning outcome,
cognitive level, exercise, dataset, duration, delivery method) at a stated 75–80% hands-on ratio;
an explicit scope decision on what is *not* taught and why; a full stand-alone 90-minute
facilitator artefact (Annex B, with a scoring guide, materials list, and completion criteria); a
12-item, 0–4 demonstrated-capability assessment instrument used identically at baseline, endline,
and the Day 30/60/90 follow-ups (Annex C); and a 90-day plan table (period, activity, owner,
evidence, success measure) built around applied output on real departmental work, not attendance
or satisfaction. Closes by arguing that capability building, reproducible documentation, peer
review, and workflow ownership are structural controls against the same coordination fragility
described in [Question 5](../Q5_Technical_Coordination/Q5_Response.docx) — stated as reducing the
likelihood of recurrence, not eliminating the operational cost of losing key staff.
