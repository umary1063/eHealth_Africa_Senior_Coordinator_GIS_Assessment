# 🎓 Question 6: Building Capability in the Counterpart Agency

*Compulsory, Part 3, maximum 6 pages excluding annexes.*

A real capability test of 21 counterpart staff found a composite score of 36/100, an objective knowledge score of 57%, almost no relationship between how confident people felt and what they could actually do, and **zero** of 21 staff with access to QGIS or ArcGIS.

**📄 [`Q6_Response.docx`](Q6_Response.docx)** covers all seven required parts:

- 🔬 Reads the evidence straight into the course design — the software-access finding becomes the **binding constraint** the whole programme lives or dies on, not a footnote, and since confidence didn't predict skill, the course is built from test results rather than from what people said they wanted
- 📐 A **4-level, 6-domain competency framework** (Annex A) defined by things you can actually observe someone do, not a vague beginner/intermediate/advanced scale
- 📅 A day-by-day **course table** (learning outcome, cognitive level, exercise, dataset, duration, delivery method), roughly 75–80% hands-on
- ✂️ States plainly what's **not** taught this round, and why
- 🛠️ One full **90-minute session written out end to end** (Annex B) — a facilitator guide, scoring rubric, materials list, and completion criteria a colleague could run without the author in the room
- 📊 A **12-item scored practical test** (0–4 per item) used identically at baseline, endline, and the Day 30/60/90 follow-ups (Annex C)
- 📈 A **90-day plan** measured by real applied work on real departmental tasks, not attendance or satisfaction

**The Q5↔Q6 link:** it closes by naming the same underlying weakness that [Question 5](../Q5_Technical_Coordination/Q5_Response.docx) describes — knowledge concentrated in individuals, with no backup, is what turns ordinary staff movement into a crisis. Capability building, documentation, peer review, and shared ownership are the structural defence against that same fragility, stated plainly as reducing the *likelihood* of a repeat, not eliminating the real cost of losing key staff.
